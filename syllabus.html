One three-hour session each week: a mix of lecture, class discussion, and in-class exercises. Dates and location TBA.

Course goals

  1. To develop working fluency with AI tools in the context of finance. We will predominantly focus on producing financial analysis with AI in the formats the financial sector actually uses and requires.
  2. To establish what AI can and cannot do reliably in financial work. We will study where its answers come from, where it fails in ways particular to financial data, and how to test its output rather than take it on faith.
  3. To develop the judgment that AI-assisted work still requires. At least in the current scheme, the burden (and the liability) of the decision still falls on humans. We will practice deciding when AI-assisted analysis supports action and defending that decision under your own name.

Learning outcomes

Upon completion of this course, students will be able to:

  • Explain where AI answers come from and connect AI tools to financial data
  • Extract structured data from financial documents with AI and verify its accuracy
  • Evaluate AI predictions, accounting for information cutoffs and contamination
  • Build a working prototype of an AI application in finance and make the case for it

Generative AI policy

AI use in this course is not only permitted but absolutely required, and part of your grade is the quality of your record of using it.

We will take a two-pronged approach to the use of generative AI in class. For the milestones and the final prototype, I will ask you to use AI as much as you can with a brief disclosure of which tools you used and how their output entered your work. Honest disclosure never lowers your grade.

On the other hand, the in-class quizzes are to be completed entirely without AI.

In the Wild: eligibility

Eligible firms are banks with more than $10 billion in assets; investment advisers or fund managers with more than $1 billion in AUM; broker-dealers; insurers; exchanges; and nationally recognized rating agencies. Eligible work includes client research, regulatory or court filings, public reports, and marketing.

Share Your Setup: four required components

  • A memory: how context survives between sessions.
  • A prompting framework you reuse across tasks.
  • A way to split work across agents or sessions and pass results between them.
  • At least two AI tools from different providers.

Attendance

This course adheres to the MBA Core attendance guidelines. Students should review and be familiar with the MBA Attendance Policy and the MBA Exam Policy.

Classroom norms

Each class begins with a short written quiz on that week’s readings. Quizzes are done on paper, without devices or AI.

Laptops stay closed unless instructed otherwise. Come prepared and contribute to discussion.

In-class lab work is individual unless specified otherwise. If groups are formed, teams have a maximum of 3–4 students. Weekly milestones, quizzes, and the final project are individual.
