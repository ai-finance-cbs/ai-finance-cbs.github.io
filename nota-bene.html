--- title: Nota Bene permalink: /nota-bene/ ---
  1. Judging an AI model runs into a joint hypothesis problem (Fama, 1970). AI output depends on two things: your input and the model's ability. When the output is bad, you cannot tell which of the two failed. For example, ask a model for a funny joke about MBA life and you get a stale one. Tell the same model what makes a joke good, and the joke gets much better.
  2. The most common complaint about AI models is that they lack "taste." Part of the reason is that people define taste differently. A model cannot meet a standard that you have not described to it.
  3. Models solved math problems early because math answers are easy to check. The same logic applies to your own work. For important tasks, use AI where you can check the output. If you cannot check it, you cannot trust it yet.
  4. Employers should hire people who have built something with AI and can show it. This can be a hackathon project, a side project, or a tool they made at work. Ask them to walk you through it, including where it broke and how they checked the output. That tells you more than a course certificate does.
  5. It has become fashionable to run writing through an AI detector and to cancel the writer if the text is flagged. This is absurd. The detectors are unreliable, and they often flag careful human writing.
  6. These resources help you keep up with AI. Techmeme has the day's tech news on one page. On Twitter/X, where most new work shows up first, follow Gavin Baker, Paul Kedrosky, Dylan Patel, Marc Rubinstein, Byrne Hobart, Joe Weisenthal, Simon Taylor, and Anton Korinek.
