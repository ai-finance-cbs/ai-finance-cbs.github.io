For employers: what kind of people should I hire for jobs that use AI?

Look for people who have built something with AI and can show it: a hackathon project, a side project, or a tool they made at work. Ask them to walk you through it, including where it broke and how they checked the output. That tells you more than a course certificate does.

What are good resources to stay on top of AI?

  • Techmeme: the day's tech news on one page.
  • Twitter/X: most of the new work and the discussion around it shows up here first. Accounts that cover AI in markets and finance:
    • Investors: Gavin Baker (Atreides; AI capex and AI stocks), Paul Kedrosky (SK Ventures; how the AI build-out is financed)
    • Analysts and writers: Dylan Patel (SemiAnalysis; chips and data-center costs), Marc Rubinstein (Net Interest; banks, private credit, AI financing), Byrne Hobart (The Diff), Joe Weisenthal (Bloomberg Odd Lots)
    • Fintech and economics: Simon Taylor (Fintech Brainfood; AI agents in banking and payments), Anton Korinek (UVA; economics of AI)
  • Podcasts:
    • Exchanges (Goldman Sachs): many recent episodes on AI and markets, such as AI debt in credit markets
    • Invest Like the Best (Patrick O’Shaughnessy): AI through an investor’s eyes
    • Unhedged (Financial Times): twice-weekly markets show that often covers AI
    • BG2Pod (Brad Gerstner and Bill Gurley): AI spending and valuations
    • AI in Financial Services (Daniel Faggella): bank and insurer executives on using AI
  • Newsletters (several are mostly paid, with free posts):
    • Net Interest (Marc Rubinstein): finance, with a series on financing the AI boom
    • The Diff (Byrne Hobart): where finance meets tech strategy
    • Paul Kedrosky: the AI build-out as a share of GDP and how it is funded
    • Epoch AI: data on the economics of AI, such as whether financing limits compute
    • SemiAnalysis (Dylan Patel): the costs behind AI infrastructure; technical
    • Fintech Brainfood (Simon Taylor): AI agents in banking and payments
